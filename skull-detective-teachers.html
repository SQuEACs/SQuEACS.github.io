Skull Detective classroom edition

Teacher guide

Skull Detective is a free science game from the SQuEACs Lab at Midwestern University for kindergarten through 6th grade. It is built on the lab's CT scans of five weasel-family cousins: the river otter, American mink, American marten, fisher, and American badger. Each grade gets its own case, tied to a science standard. Every line is read aloud and shown as a caption.

Teacher settings

On the classroom edition's title screen, press and hold the gear for about a second (or Tab to it and press Enter).

  • Lock a grade so every student opens the same case.
  • Slower voice for younger students or English learners.
  • Captions show the spoken words under the game.
  • Clear this tablet's progress between classes.

To open a grade from a link, add ?grade=k, ?grade=g1, up to ?grade=g6 to the classroom page's address.

Accessibility

  • Every instruction, question, and answer is spoken, and shown as a caption.
  • No timers and no penalties. Wrong answers bring a hint.
  • Large buttons, high-contrast outlines, and keyboard play (Tab to move, Enter to choose). Sliders work with arrow keys.
  • Animations stop when the device is set to reduce motion.
  • 3D prints of the skulls make good tactile copies for students with low vision.

The science

The five animals are close relatives in the weasel family (Mustelidae). Because they share an ancestor, the differences between their skulls point to how each one lives: the otter's flat, wide head and big whisker-nerve openings for hunting in water, the marten's light skull for climbing, the fisher's tall crest for strong jaw muscles, and the badger's wide, triangular skull for digging. Skull drawings follow each species' real proportions, and the sizes are typical adult measurements. The family tree follows published DNA studies: the badger branched off first, martens and fishers are close cousins, and otters and mink are close cousins.

Privacy

  • No accounts and no student names. The game asks for nothing.
  • Progress saves only in the browser on that device.
  • Speech is made by the device's own voice. The page loads its fonts from Google Fonts.
