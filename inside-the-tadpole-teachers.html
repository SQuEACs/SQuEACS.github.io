Inside the Tadpole classroom edition

Teacher guide

Inside the Tadpole is a free science game from the SQuEACs Lab at Midwestern University for kindergarten through 6th grade. It is built on the lab's iodine-stained CT scans of tadpoles and fish, which show soft tissues like the brain, gills, heart, gut, and muscles. Students tap parts on a see-through tadpole and slide through it slice by slice, the way a CT scanner images a body. Every line is read aloud and shown as a caption.

Teacher settings

On the classroom edition's title screen, press and hold the gear for about a second (or Tab to it and press Enter).

  • Lock a grade so every student opens the same mission.
  • Slower voice for younger students or English learners.
  • Captions show the spoken words under the game.
  • Clear this tablet's progress between classes.

To open a grade from a link, add ?grade=k, ?grade=g1, up to ?grade=g6 to the classroom page's address.

Accessibility

  • Every instruction, question, and answer is spoken, and shown as a caption.
  • No timers and no penalties. Wrong answers name the part that was tapped and give a hint.
  • Large buttons, high-contrast outlines, and keyboard play. The slice slider announces which parts are in the current slice for screen readers.
  • Animations stop when the device is set to reduce motion.

The science

Bone shows up well in an ordinary CT scan, but soft tissue does not. Soaking a specimen in an iodine stain makes muscles, nerves, and organs show up too, so a CT scan can show a tadpole's brain, gills, and coiled gut without dissection. The drawings in the game are simplified from these scans. A tadpole's gut is several times longer than its body because algae are hard to digest. During metamorphosis the gut shortens as the froglet switches to eating insects, the gills give way to lungs, and the tail is absorbed.

Privacy

  • No accounts and no student names. The game asks for nothing.
  • Progress saves only in the browser on that device.
  • Speech is made by the device's own voice. The page loads its fonts from Google Fonts.
