Mammal Mystery classroom edition

Teacher guide

Mammal Mystery is a free detective game from the SQuEACs Lab at Midwestern University for pre-K through 2nd grade. Detective Ollie, an otter, reads clues out loud. Each round is an expedition: students travel across a map to three habitats, find the animal hiding behind bushes, rocks, or snow, discover its superpower, and earn stickers and detective ranks. Nothing on screen needs to be read.

How an expedition works

1Map. Ollie walks to the next habitat on the expedition map. Students tap the glowing stop to travel.
2Hide and seek. Several animals peek out from behind bushes, rocks, grass, reeds, or snow. Ollie shows a close-up clue through a magnifying glass and reads it aloud, for example "My big pointy ears can hear a mouse under the snow." Students tap the hiding spot of the animal that matches. A wrong pick pops up, says "Not me!", and brings the next clue. There is no penalty and no timer.
3Case file. The right animal jumps out and dances. A case file opens with a picture and two to four facts for their grade, all read aloud. Tap any fact to hear it again.
4Superpower. One body part glows. Students tap it and watch it work: the kangaroo hops, the elephant sprays water, the fox's ears pick up a mouse under the snow. Ollie explains how that adaptation helps, with more detail at each grade, and students earn a superpower sticker.
5Grade bonus. K taps two body parts. 1st grade picks the animal's habitat. 2nd grade decides carnivore, herbivore, or omnivore.
6Bonus game. Halfway through, a quick shadow match (pre-K and K) or footprint match (1st and 2nd). Both games are also on the menu under the paw.
7Stickers and ranks. Every animal found goes in the sticker book. Detective ranks go from Rookie to Master Detective as students find more animals, with a celebration at each new rank.

What each grade does

Pre-K

  • Names 12 mammals.
  • Clues are about looks: color, a mane, a long neck, plus a shadow.
  • Three animals hiding at a time.
  • Coloring: tap a crayon, then tap the picture. The voice names each color.

K

  • Body parts and what they do: ears hear, whiskers feel, webbed feet swim.
  • "Detective check": tap two body parts on the animal.
  • Four animals hiding.
  • Coloring: the voice names the color and the body part, like "Blue ears!"

1st

  • Habitats: river, forest, grassland, desert, ice.
  • How body parts help an animal live in its home.
  • Pick the habitat after each mystery.
  • Coloring: the voice names the color and the body part, and reads a 1st-grade fact when the picture is done.

2nd

  • Teeth as evidence for diet: the teeth clue always comes first, and the teeth are shown again when students decide carnivore, herbivore, or omnivore.
  • Family secrets: which two animals are closer cousins, then the whole family tree.
  • Tap any dot on the family tree for a card about that group: its teeth, more facts, and relatives that are not in the game (seals, whales, koalas, and more).
  • Four animals hiding, with harder clues.
  • Coloring is here too, with 2nd-grade facts.

A 15-minute lesson

2 minHook. Put on a pretend detective hat. "Detectives use clues to solve mysteries. Today we'll find hidden animals."
3 minModel. Solve one mystery together on the board. Think aloud: "Big ears? Which animals have big ears?"
6 minPlay. Pairs share a device and take turns: one taps a hiding spot, the other taps the superpower.
4 minShare. Each pair names one animal from their sticker book and its superpower. Then everyone acts it out: hop like a kangaroo, listen like a fox. Hand out that animal's coloring page.

Coloring and activity sheets

  • A cover page with a field guide checklist of all 12 animals.
  • A coloring page for each animal, with its name in big letters to color and a fact to read aloud.
  • Pre-K and K: color by body part.
  • 1st grade: draw a line from each animal to its home.
  • 2nd grade: Teeth detective, and a cut-and-glue family tree. Answers are printed small and upside down.
  • All grades: draw your own mystery mammal and write three clues.

Teacher settings

On the classroom edition's title screen, press and hold the gear for about a second. The press-and-hold keeps young students out.

  • Voice: the game picks the warmest, most natural voice on each device. Choose a different one here and tap "Try it" to hear it.
  • Grade: lock the whole class to one grade, or let students choose.
  • Mysteries per round: 3, 5, 8, or all 12 stops on the expedition map.
  • Slower voice for pre-K or English learners.
  • Captions: show the spoken words at the bottom of the screen.
  • Clear this device's sticker book (animals, superpowers, and rank) between classes.
No voice? The game uses the device's built-in text-to-speech. If nothing plays, check the volume and mute switch, and on Chromebooks make sure a voice is installed under Settings → Accessibility → Text-to-speech. Want a warmer voice? On iPads and Macs, download "Ava (Premium)" or "Zoe (Premium)" under Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices → English. On Windows, open the game in Edge for its natural voices. Families can change the voice too: press and hold the gear on the game's title screen.

The science

The 12 animals cover six continents. Seven are carnivorans, the group our lab studies (river otter, raccoon, red fox, lion, polar bear, giant panda, meerkat), and five are other mammals (rabbit, kangaroo, elephant, giraffe, camel). The drawings are simplified cartoons. The family tree shows widely accepted relationships among these 12 animals only, so it skips many branches. The giant panda is a good discussion point: it is a carnivoran that eats almost only bamboo, which shows that a group's name doesn't always match every member's diet.

Privacy

  • No accounts and no student names. The game asks for nothing.
  • The sticker book and rank save only in the browser on that device.
  • The classroom edition does not send play counts to the lab.
  • Speech is made by the device's own voice. The page loads its fonts from Google Fonts.

Check these details against your school or district rules for student use of web tools.
