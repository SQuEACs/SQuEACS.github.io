Open Science / Skull Detective

Skull Detective

A CT scan science game for kindergarten to 6th grade. Meet five mustelids (members of the weasel family) and solve a case for your grade: find skull parts, match skulls to habitats, learn how CT scanners work and graph your own measurements, follow messages from the senses to the brain, read the teeth, and build a phylogenetic tree. Everything is read aloud.

How to play: pick your grade and open the case. Every case starts by meeting the animals. Each grade has its own mission: find skull parts (K), classify skulls by job (1st), match skulls to habitats (2nd), measure skulls and make a graph (3rd), follow the senses to the brain (4th), learn the kinds of teeth and follow the energy (5th), and build a phylogenetic tree (6th). Tap any highlighted science word to see and hear what it means. Everything is read aloud and shown as captions. Tap the speaker to hear it again. Works with touch, a mouse, or a keyboard (Tab and Enter).

What each grade learns

  • K: skull parts and what they do (K-LS1-1).
  • 1st: sorting animals into groups, and copying nature to solve problems (1-LS1-1).
  • 2nd: habitats and the variety of life in them (2-LS4-1).
  • 3rd: measuring, graphing, and arguing from evidence about survival (3-LS4-2, 3-LS4-3).
  • 4th: senses, the brain, and how bodies fit habitats (4-LS1-1, 4-LS1-2).
  • 5th: teeth, diets, and energy in food chains (5-LS2-1, 5-PS3-1).
  • 6th: shared features and family trees (MS-LS4-2).

The skulls

The SQuEACs Lab CT-scanned the skulls of five North American mustelids (the weasel family): river otter, American mink, American marten, fisher, and American badger. They are close relatives, so every difference between their skulls is a clue to how each one lives.

For teachers

The classroom edition lets you lock a grade, slow the voice, and turn captions on or off. The teacher guide has a lesson plan for each grade.

Teacher guide

Skull drawings follow each species' real proportions and will be joined by the lab's CT scan images. Skull sizes are typical adult measurements. The phylogenetic tree follows published studies of the weasel family. Progress is saved only in this browser.
