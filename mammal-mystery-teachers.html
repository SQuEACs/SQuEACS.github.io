Mammal Mystery classroom edition

Teacher guide

Mammal Mystery is a free detective game from the SQuEACs Lab at Midwestern University for pre-K through 2nd grade. Detective Ollie, an otter, reads clues out loud. Each round is an expedition: students travel across a map to three habitats, find the animal hiding behind bushes, rocks, or snow, discover its superpower, and earn stickers and detective ranks. Nothing on screen needs to be read.

How an expedition works

1Map. Ollie walks to the next habitat on the expedition map. Students tap the glowing stop to travel.
2Hide and seek. Several animals peek out from behind bushes, rocks, grass, reeds, or snow. Ollie shows a close-up clue through a magnifying glass and reads it aloud, for example "My big pointy ears can hear a mouse under the snow." Students tap the hiding spot of the animal that matches. A wrong pick pops up, says "Not me!", and brings the next clue. There is no penalty and no timer.
3Case file. The right animal jumps out and dances. A case file opens with a picture and two to four facts for their grade, all read aloud. Tap any fact to hear it again.
4Superpower. One body part glows. Students tap it and watch it work: the kangaroo hops, the elephant sprays water, the fox's ears pick up a mouse under the snow. Ollie explains how that adaptation helps, with more detail at each grade, and students earn a superpower sticker.
5Grade bonus. K taps two body parts. 1st grade picks the animal's habitat. 2nd grade decides carnivore, herbivore, or omnivore.
6Bonus game. Halfway through, a quick shadow match (pre-K and K) or footprint match (1st and 2nd). Both games are also on the menu under the paw.
7Stickers and ranks. After each right answer a Next arrow appears; tapping anywhere moves on without waiting for Ollie to finish talking. Every animal found goes in the sticker book. Detective ranks go from Rookie to Master Detective as students find more animals, with a celebration at each new rank. A Start over button on the menu gives the next student a fresh sticker book (hidden when a grade is locked; use the reset in settings instead).
8Level 2. Once a device's sticker book has all 12 animals, Level 2 opens with 12 surprising mammals: red panda, sloth, armadillo, platypus, hedgehog, fruit bat, koala, okapi, pangolin, walrus, gray wolf, and snow leopard. They bring two new habitats (rainforest and mountains) and new superpowers, such as the platypus's electric bill and the pangolin's scaly armor. Until then, the sticker book shows a locked 2 that says "Find all 12 to unlock"; tapping it tells students how many animals are left. Students switch levels with the 1 and 2 buttons on the menu and in the sticker book. To open Level 2 right away, use Open Level 2 now in the teacher settings.

What each grade does

Pre-K

  • Names 12 mammals.
  • Clues are about looks: color, a mane, a long neck, plus a shadow.
  • Three animals hiding at a time.
  • Coloring: tap a crayon, then tap the picture. The voice names each color.

K

  • Body parts and what they do: ears hear, whiskers feel, webbed feet swim.
  • "Detective check": tap two body parts on the animal.
  • Four animals hiding.
  • Coloring: the voice names the color and the body part, like "Blue ears!"

1st

  • Habitats: river, forest, grassland, desert, ice, plus rainforest and mountains in Level 2.
  • How body parts help an animal live in its home.
  • Pick the habitat after each mystery.
  • Coloring: the voice names the color and the body part, and reads a 1st-grade fact when the picture is done.

2nd

  • Teeth as evidence for diet: the teeth clue always comes first, and the teeth are shown again when students decide carnivore, herbivore, or omnivore.
  • Family secrets: which two animals are closer cousins, then the whole family tree.
  • Tap any dot on the family tree for a card about that group: its teeth, more facts, and relatives that are not in the game (seals, whales, koalas, and more).
  • Four animals hiding, with harder clues.
  • At Level 2 the cousins questions and the family tree switch to the Level 2 animals.
  • Coloring is here too, with 2nd-grade facts.

Standards connections

The game supports these Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) performance expectations, checked against the official text at nextgenscience.org. States that adopted or adapted NGSS, including Arizona, use the same ideas but may number them differently. Like any single activity, the game builds toward a performance expectation; students meet it fully through the talk, drawing, and writing that follow.

Looking ahead. The 2nd-grade teeth and family tree activities build early ideas for later standards: 4-LS1-1 (animals have internal and external structures that support survival, growth, behavior, and reproduction) and MS-LS4-2 (using similarities and differences in anatomy to infer evolutionary relationships). They are previews, not grade-level goals.

Pre-K. NGSS starts at kindergarten. For pre-K, the game fits early learning goals for science found in most state early learning standards, including Arizona's: observing, describing, and comparing living things, and using new words for what children notice.

A 15-minute lesson

2 minHook. Put on a pretend detective hat. "Detectives use clues to solve mysteries. Today we'll find hidden animals."
3 minModel. Solve one mystery together on the board. Think aloud: "Big ears? Which animals have big ears?"
6 minPlay. Pairs share a device and take turns: one taps a hiding spot, the other taps the superpower.
4 minShare. Each pair names one animal from their sticker book and its superpower. Then everyone acts it out: hop like a kangaroo, listen like a fox. Hand out that animal's coloring page or the detective record sheet.

Extension: superpower design challenge (1st grade, 20 minutes)

This finishes the 1-LS1-1 standard. After an expedition, each pair picks one superpower from their sticker book and uses paper, tape, cups, or craft sticks to make something that helps people the same way. Examples: a paper-cone "fox ear" that makes quiet sounds louder, scaly paper armor like a pangolin's, flippers like an otter's webbed feet. Pairs share: "The animal uses its ___ to ___. Our invention helps people ___."

Common misconceptions to listen for

Students may sayWhat the game shows
"Only furry pets and farm animals are mammals."Mammals have fur or hair and feed their babies milk. Whales, bats, and the spiky hedgehog and scaly pangolin are mammals too, and the platypus even lays eggs.
"The animal grew its superpower because it needed it."Animals are born with their body parts. A fox doesn't grow big ears by trying to hear; its ears came from its parents. (For older students: over many generations, foxes with better hearing raised more young.)
"A bat is a bird" or "A walrus is a fish."Looks and home don't decide the group. Bats have fur and feed their babies milk, and walruses breathe air and nurse their calves.
"Pandas eat bamboo, so they're not in the meat-eater family."The giant panda is a carnivoran, the group with otters, foxes, and lions. A group's name doesn't always match every member's diet; its sharp-edged teeth and its relatives show where it belongs.
"Animals that look alike are close cousins."The red panda looks a bit like a raccoon and eats bamboo like the giant panda, but it is not a bear. The family tree is built from many clues, not looks alone.

Coloring and activity sheets

  • A cover page with a field guide checklist of all 12 animals.
  • A coloring page for each animal, with its name in big letters to color and a fact to read aloud.
  • Pre-K and K: color by body part.
  • 1st grade: draw a line from each animal to its home.
  • 2nd grade: Teeth detective, and a cut-and-glue family tree. Answers are printed small and upside down.
  • Level 2: a second cover and checklist, a coloring page for each of the 12 Level 2 animals, a Level 2 "Who lives here?" (mountains, rainforest, ice, river), and a Level 2 Teeth detective.
  • All grades: draw your own mystery mammal and write three clues.

Coloring in the game: each time a student opens an animal's coloring page it starts blank. Tapping the check mark saves the picture, and the device keeps up to 3 saved versions of each animal (the oldest is put away when a 4th is saved). Saved versions show as small pictures beside the page; tap one to keep coloring it.

Teacher settings

On the classroom edition's title screen, press and hold the gear for about a second. The press-and-hold keeps young students out.

  • Voice: the game picks the warmest, most natural voice on each device. Choose a different one here and tap "Try it" to hear it.
  • Grade: lock the whole class to one grade, or let students choose.
  • Mysteries per round: 3, 5, 8, or all 12 stops on the expedition map.
  • Slower voice for pre-K or English learners.
  • Captions: show the spoken words at the bottom of the screen.
  • Clear this device's sticker book (animals, superpowers, rank, and Level 2) between classes.
  • Open Level 2 now lets students play the 12 surprising mammals without finding the first 12 again.
No voice? The game uses the device's built-in text-to-speech. If nothing plays, check the volume and mute switch, and on Chromebooks make sure a voice is installed under Settings → Accessibility → Text-to-speech. Want a warmer voice? On iPads and Macs, download "Ava (Premium)" or "Zoe (Premium)" under Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices → English. On Windows, open the game in Edge for its natural voices. Families can change the voice too: press and hold the gear on the game's title screen.

The science

The 12 animals cover six continents. Seven are carnivorans, the group our lab studies (river otter, raccoon, red fox, lion, polar bear, giant panda, meerkat), and five are other mammals (rabbit, kangaroo, elephant, giraffe, camel). The drawings are simplified cartoons. The family tree shows widely accepted relationships among these 12 animals only, so it skips many branches. The giant panda is a good discussion point: it is a carnivoran that eats almost only bamboo, which shows that a group's name doesn't always match every member's diet.

Level 2 adds 12 mammals chosen to surprise: an egg-laying mammal (platypus), the only mammals that truly fly (bats), mammals with armor (armadillo) or scales (pangolin), a marsupial that is not a bear (koala), the giraffe's forest cousin (okapi), and carnivorans that live on ice, in mountains, and in forests (walrus, snow leopard, gray wolf, red panda). Good discussion points: the platypus and pangolin have no teeth as adults, the red panda is a bamboo-eating carnivoran like the giant panda but not a bear, and the pangolin's closest living relatives are the carnivorans. At Level 2, the 2nd-grade family secrets and family tree use the 12 Level 2 animals, from the egg-laying platypus at the base to the walrus and red panda, which are closer cousins to each other than either is to the wolf.

Privacy

  • No accounts and no student names. The game asks for nothing.
  • The sticker book and rank save only in the browser on that device.
  • The classroom edition does not send play counts to the lab.
  • Speech is made by the device's own voice. The page loads its fonts from Google Fonts.
